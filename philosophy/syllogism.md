# Syllogism

## Definition
A syllogism is a form of deductive reasoning in which a conclusion is drawn from two given or assumed propositions (premises), each sharing a common term with the conclusion.

## Key Idea
Introduced systematically by Aristotle, the syllogism is a cornerstone of classical logic. If the premises are true and the argument's structure is valid, the conclusion must also be true.

## Details

### Structure
A classic categorical syllogism has three parts:
- **Major premise** - a general statement.
- **Minor premise** - a more specific statement.
- **Conclusion** - derived from the two premises.

**Example:**
- Major premise: All men are mortal.
- Minor premise: Socrates is a man.
- Conclusion: Therefore, Socrates is mortal.

### Types
- **Categorical Syllogism** - deals with categories/classes (as in the example above).
- **Conditional (Hypothetical) Syllogism** - based on "if-then" statements.
- **Disjunctive Syllogism** - based on "either-or" statements.

### Validity vs. Truth
- A syllogism can be **logically valid** (the conclusion follows from the structure) even if the premises are factually **false**.
- A syllogism is **sound** only when it is valid **and** all premises are true.

## Significance
- Forms the foundation of formal deductive logic.
- Widely used in philosophy, law, mathematics, and rhetoric to construct and evaluate arguments.
- Common syllogistic fallacies (e.g., undistributed middle) highlight where reasoning can go wrong despite seeming plausible.

## Related Concepts
- Syllogism is a core method within [[deductive-reasoning|Deductive Reasoning]], the broader logical framework it belongs to.
- It contrasts with [[inductive-reasoning|Inductive Reasoning]], which draws general conclusions from specific observations rather than guaranteed logical structure.
- It is foundational to [[formal-logic|Formal Logic]], the mathematical study of valid inference.
- Errors in syllogistic structure are studied under [[logical-fallacy|Logical Fallacy]], which catalogs common ways arguments fail despite appearing valid.
