# Sexual Selection

## Definition
Sexual selection is a mode of natural selection in which individuals with certain heritable traits are more successful at attracting mates or competing for reproductive opportunities, causing those traits to become more common over generations.

## Key Idea
Unlike ordinary natural selection, which favors traits that improve survival, sexual selection favors traits that improve reproductive success - even when those traits carry a survival cost (e.g., bright plumage that attracts predators as well as mates).

## Details

### Mechanisms
- **Intersexual Selection (Mate Choice)** - one sex (often females) selects mates based on specific traits, favoring exaggerated ornaments or displays.
- **Intrasexual Selection (Mate Competition)** - individuals of the same sex (often males) compete directly for access to mates, favoring weapons, size, or aggression.

### Examples
- Elaborate plumage and courtship displays, such as a peacock's tail.
- Physical weaponry used in combat between rivals, such as antlers in deer or horns in beetles.
- Evidence suggests some [[dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]] evolved elaborate crests, frills, and display structures that may have been shaped by sexual selection rather than survival alone.

## Significance
- A specialized case of [[natural-selection|Natural Selection]], explaining traits that seem maladaptive for survival but boost mating success.
- Helps explain sexual dimorphism - physical differences between males and females of the same species.
- Offers insight into the evolution of extravagant or costly traits across many groups, from birds and mammals to extinct lineages studied in [[paleontology|Paleontology]].

## Limitations

### Criticisms
- Distinguishing sexual selection from ordinary natural selection can be difficult, since traits often serve both survival and mating functions.
- Inferring sexual selection in extinct species (e.g., dinosaurs) relies on indirect fossil evidence and comparison with living animals, making conclusions harder to confirm.

## Related Concepts
- [[natural-selection|Natural Selection]] is the broader process of which sexual selection is a specialized subtype.
- [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]] is the overall process shaped in part by sexual selection pressures.
- [[dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]] provide notable examples where display structures and ornamentation are hypothesized to result from sexual selection.
- [[paleontology|Paleontology]] supplies the fossil evidence used to investigate sexual selection in extinct species.
