# Paleontology

## Definition
Paleontology is the scientific study of the history of life on Earth through the examination of fossils - the preserved remains or traces of ancient organisms.

## Key Idea
By analyzing fossils and the rock layers (strata) in which they are found, paleontologists reconstruct how life has changed over deep time, providing direct physical evidence for evolutionary history.

## Details

### Subfields
- **Vertebrate Paleontology** - study of fossil animals with backbones, including [[dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]] and early mammals.
- **Invertebrate Paleontology** - study of fossil animals without backbones (e.g., mollusks, trilobites).
- **Paleobotany** - study of fossil plants.
- **Micropaleontology** - study of microscopic fossils (e.g., foraminifera, pollen).

### Methods
- **Stratigraphy** - analyzing rock layers to determine the relative age of fossils.
- **Radiometric Dating** - using the decay of radioactive isotopes to assign absolute ages to rocks and fossils.
- **Comparative Anatomy** - comparing fossilized structures to living organisms to infer relationships and function.

## Significance
- Provides direct physical evidence for [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]], documenting how species have changed and diversified over time.
- Helps refine [[taxonomy|Taxonomy]] by revealing extinct lineages and clarifying evolutionary relationships between organisms.
- Informs understanding of mass extinctions, past climates, and the origins of major groups (e.g., the transition from fish to land vertebrates).

## Limitations

### Criticisms
- The fossil record is incomplete - many organisms, especially soft-bodied ones, rarely fossilize.
- Dating methods carry margins of error and depend on the quality and context of the samples found.
- Gaps in the fossil record can lead to competing interpretations of evolutionary transitions.

## Related Concepts
- [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]] is the process paleontology provides physical evidence for through the fossil record.
- [[taxonomy|Taxonomy]] relies on paleontological findings to classify extinct organisms and refine evolutionary relationships.
- [[natural-selection|Natural Selection]] is often inferred from patterns of change observed across fossil lineages.
- [[dinosaurs|Dinosaurs]] are among the most iconic subjects of paleontological study, illustrating extinction, evolutionary radiation, and deep time.
