# Natural Evolution

## Definition
Natural evolution is the process by which populations of organisms change over successive generations through variation, heredity, and differential survival and reproduction.

## Key Idea
Heritable traits that improve survival or reproduction become more common in a population over time, while less advantageous traits become rarer. Over long timescales, this process can produce new species and dramatic adaptations.

## Details

### Mechanisms
- **Mutation** - introduces new genetic variation into a population.
- **Natural Selection** - favors traits that improve fitness in a given environment.
- **Genetic Drift** - random changes in allele frequencies, especially significant in small populations.
- **Gene Flow** - the transfer of genetic variation between populations via migration.

### Evidence
- Fossil record showing gradual change over time.
- Comparative anatomy and embryology across species.
- Molecular biology (DNA/protein similarities between related species).
- Observed evolution in real time (e.g., antibiotic resistance, pesticide resistance).

## Significance
- Explains the diversity and unity of life on Earth.
- Forms the foundational theory of modern biology, first articulated by Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace.
- Underpins fields such as genetics, ecology, and medicine.
