# Genetic Diversity

## Definition
Genetic diversity refers to the total number and variety of genetic characteristics present within a population, species, or group of organisms.

## Key Idea
Higher genetic diversity generally increases a population's ability to adapt to changing environments, resist disease, and avoid the harmful effects of inbreeding, because a broader range of traits is available for selection to act on.

## Details

### Sources
- **Mutation** - introduces new alleles into a population.
- **Recombination** - reshuffles existing genetic material during sexual reproduction.
- **Gene Flow** - migration introduces new alleles from other populations.

## Significance
A key indicator of ecosystem and species health. Provides the raw material that [[natural-selection|Natural Selection]] acts upon during [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]].

### Importance
- **Adaptability** - populations with more variation are better equipped to survive environmental change, disease, or new predators.
- **Resilience** - reduces the risk of extinction from a single catastrophic event affecting all individuals equally.
- **Agriculture and Conservation** - critical for crop breeding, livestock health, and preserving endangered species.

## Limitations

### Threats
- Habitat loss and population fragmentation reducing gene flow.
- Small population sizes leading to inbreeding and genetic drift.
- Selective breeding practices that narrow genetic variation (e.g., monoculture crops).

## Related Concepts
- [[mutation|Mutation]] is a primary source of new genetic diversity within a population.
- [[natural-selection|Natural Selection]] acts on genetic diversity to drive adaptation.
- [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]] depends on genetic diversity as its underlying raw material.
