# Mutation

## Definition
A mutation is a permanent change in the nucleotide sequence of an organism's DNA (or RNA in some viruses). Mutations can occur in genes or in non-coding regions and range from a single base change to large-scale chromosomal alterations.

## Key Idea
Mutations are the raw material of genetic variation. They can arise spontaneously (e.g., replication errors) or be induced by external factors (mutagens), and their effects range from neutral to beneficial to harmful.

## Details

### Types
- **Point Mutations** - changes affecting a single nucleotide (substitution, insertion, deletion).
- **Frameshift Mutations** - insertions/deletions that shift the reading frame of the genetic code.
- **Silent Mutations** - do not change the resulting amino acid sequence.
- **Missense Mutations** - change one amino acid for another.
- **Nonsense Mutations** - introduce a premature stop codon.
- **Chromosomal Mutations** - large-scale changes such as duplications, deletions, inversions, or translocations.

### Causes
- **Spontaneous** - errors during DNA replication or repair.
- **Induced** - exposure to mutagens (radiation, certain chemicals, viruses).

## Significance
- Primary source of genetic diversity within populations.
- Drives evolution through natural selection acting on variation.
- Underlies many genetic diseases and cancers when mutations affect critical genes.

### Effects
- **Neutral** - no significant effect on the organism (e.g., synonymous substitutions).
- **Beneficial** - can confer an advantage, contributing to adaptation and evolution.
- **Harmful** - can disrupt protein function, cause disease, or be lethal.

## Related Concepts
- Mutation is fundamental for [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]], serving as the primary source of heritable variation that natural selection acts upon.
- It is the biological inspiration behind [[bio-inspired-computing|Bio-inspired Computing]], particularly the mutation operator used in genetic algorithms.
- It relates to [[natural-selection|Natural Selection]], which determines whether a mutation persists, spreads, or disappears in a population.
- It underlies [[genetic-diversity|Genetic Diversity]], a key factor in a population's resilience and adaptability.
