	# Taxonomy

## Definition
Taxonomy is the branch of biology concerned with identifying, naming, describing, and classifying organisms into a structured hierarchy of groups based on shared characteristics.

## Key Idea
Taxonomy organizes the diversity of life into nested categories, allowing organisms to be systematically named and related to one another based on shared traits, evolutionary history, or genetic similarity.

## Details

### Hierarchy of Classification
From broadest to most specific, the standard taxonomic ranks are:
- **Domain**
- **Kingdom**
- **Phylum**
- **Class**
- **Order**
- **Family**
- **Genus**
- **Species**

### Naming Convention
- Organisms are named using **binomial nomenclature**, established by Carl Linnaeus - a two-part Latin name combining genus and species (e.g., *Homo sapiens*).
- The genus is capitalized, the species is lowercase, and both are italicized in formal writing.

### Modern Approaches
- **Traditional (Linnaean) Taxonomy** - groups organisms by observable physical characteristics.
- **Phylogenetic Taxonomy (Cladistics)** - groups organisms based on evolutionary relationships and shared ancestry, often informed by DNA analysis.

## Significance
- Provides a universal, standardized system for identifying and communicating about organisms across languages and disciplines.
- Reflects evolutionary relationships, helping trace how species are related through [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]].
- Essential for biodiversity research, conservation efforts, and medicine (e.g., identifying pathogens).
- Extinct organisms uncovered through [[paleontology|Paleontology]] are classified within the same taxonomic framework, helping place them relative to living species.

## Limitations

### Criticisms
- Traditional classifications based on physical traits can misrepresent true evolutionary relationships (convergent evolution can make unrelated species look similar).
- Species boundaries are sometimes ambiguous, especially with hybridization or asexual organisms.
- Taxonomic classifications are periodically revised as new genetic evidence emerges, causing instability in naming.

## Related Concepts
- [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]] provides the underlying evolutionary relationships that modern phylogenetic taxonomy aims to reflect.
- [[genetic-diversity|Genetic Diversity]] is often used as evidence to refine or revise taxonomic classifications.
- [[natural-selection|Natural Selection]] drives the divergence of species that taxonomy ultimately catalogs.
- [[paleontology|Paleontology]] supplies fossil evidence used to classify extinct organisms and reconstruct evolutionary lineages.
