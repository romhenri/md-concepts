# Scalar

## Definition
A scalar is a single numerical quantity, fully described by magnitude alone, with no associated direction or additional structure.

## Key Idea
In linear algebra, a scalar is the base case of the tensor hierarchy: a quantity with zero indices, as opposed to a vector (one index) or matrix (two indices). Scaling a vector or matrix by a scalar multiplies every one of its components by that single number.

## Details

### Examples
- Temperature, mass, and speed are physical quantities represented as scalars.
- In a vector space over the real numbers, the scalars are the real numbers themselves; over the complex numbers, scalars are complex numbers.

### Role in Operations
- **Scalar Multiplication** - multiplying a vector or matrix by a scalar scales every component uniformly.
- **Dot Product** - the product of two vectors of the same size is a scalar, collapsing directional information into a single magnitude.

## Significance
- Forms the field of coefficients (e.g., real or complex numbers) that a vector space is defined over.
- Provides the base case of the tensor hierarchy, with a [[vector|Vector]] as the next step up.

## Related Concepts
- [[vector|Vector]] generalizes a scalar by adding direction, becoming an ordered collection of scalars.
- [[tensor|Tensor]] treats a scalar as its simplest case, a rank-0 tensor with no indices.
