# Artificial Intelligence

## Definition
Artificial intelligence is the field of computer science concerned with building systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as reasoning, perception, language understanding, and decision-making.

## Key Idea
The field spans a wide spectrum from hand-coded rule-based systems to modern approaches that learn behavior from data. Alan Turing proposed an early operational test for machine intelligence in 1950, and the term "artificial intelligence" itself was coined at the 1956 Dartmouth Conference.

## Details

### Approaches
- **Symbolic AI** - encodes knowledge and reasoning as explicit rules and logic (expert systems, knowledge graphs).
- **Statistical and Learning-based AI** - systems that learn patterns from data rather than following hand-coded rules, dominated in practice by [[machine-learning|Machine Learning]].
- **Search and Planning** - explores a space of possible actions or states to find a path to a goal (used in game-playing agents, robotics).

### Subfields
- **Natural Language Processing** - understanding and generating human language.
- **Computer Vision** - interpreting and reasoning about visual input.
- **Robotics** - perceiving and acting in a physical environment.

### Levels of Capability
- **Narrow AI** - performs a specific task well (e.g., image classification, translation); all currently deployed AI systems fall in this category.
- **General AI** - a hypothetical system with human-level, cross-domain reasoning and adaptability, not yet achieved.

## Significance
- Powers modern applications in search, translation, recommendation, autonomous vehicles, and scientific discovery.
- [[machine-learning|Machine Learning]] is the dominant technique through which most contemporary AI systems are built.
- Relies on [[optimization|Optimization]] to fit and tune the models underlying most learning-based systems.
- Implemented and deployed through [[programming|Programming]] languages and frameworks.

## Limitations

### Criticisms
- Narrow AI systems lack genuine understanding and can fail unpredictably outside their training distribution.
- Raises open questions about bias, safety, job displacement, and accountability as capabilities grow.
- Progress is often driven by scale (data and compute) rather than fundamental theoretical advances, which some argue is not a sustainable path to general intelligence.

## Related Concepts
- [[machine-learning|Machine Learning]] is the primary modern approach for building AI systems, treating intelligence as something learned from data rather than hand-coded.
- [[optimization|Optimization]] supplies the mathematical machinery used to train the models most AI systems depend on.
- [[programming|Programming]] is the practical medium through which AI systems are implemented and deployed.
- [[bio-inspired-computing|Bio-inspired Computing]] overlaps with AI as a subfield exploring nature-derived approaches to problem-solving, such as evolutionary algorithms and neural networks.
