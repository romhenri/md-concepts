# Bio-inspired Computing

## Definition
Bio-inspired computing is a field of computer science that draws on principles, structures, and processes observed in biological systems to design algorithms, architectures, and computational models.

## Key Idea
Nature has evolved highly efficient solutions to complex problems (optimization, adaptation, self-organization, robustness). Bio-inspired computing translates these mechanisms into computational techniques rather than relying purely on deductive, hand-engineered logic.

## Details

### Common Approaches
- **Evolutionary Algorithms** - genetic algorithms, genetic programming, and evolutionary strategies that mimic natural selection and mutation.
- **Neural Networks** - inspired by the structure and signaling of biological neurons.
- **Swarm Intelligence** - ant colony optimization, particle swarm optimization, modeled on collective behavior of social insects/animals.
- **Artificial Immune Systems** - inspired by the adaptive immune response for anomaly/intrusion detection.
- **Cellular Automata** - models based on simple local rules producing complex emergent behavior, inspired by cell growth patterns.
- **DNA/Molecular Computing** - using biochemical processes (e.g., DNA strand interactions) to perform computation.

## Significance

### Applications
- Optimization problems (scheduling, routing, resource allocation)
- Machine learning and pattern recognition
- Robotics (swarm robotics, adaptive control)
- Cybersecurity (anomaly detection)
- Generative design and art

### Advantages
- Robustness and adaptability to dynamic/noisy environments
- Often effective for NP-hard or poorly-defined optimization problems
- Parallelism naturally mirrors biological processes

## Limitations

### Criticisms
- Can be computationally expensive
- Results are sometimes hard to interpret or guarantee (no formal proofs of optimality)
- Risk of "biology-washing" - using biological metaphors loosely without genuine algorithmic benefit

## Related Concepts
- Bio-inspired computing is used for [[machine-learning|Machine Learning]], particularly in optimizing model architectures and hyperparameters.
- It draws directly on the biological concept of [[mutation|Mutation]], which inspires the mutation operator in genetic algorithms.
- It overlaps with [[artificial-intelligence|Artificial Intelligence]] as a subfield exploring nature-derived approaches to problem-solving.
- [[emergence|Emergence]] is a key related concept, since many bio-inspired systems (e.g., swarm intelligence, cellular automata) produce complex behavior from simple local rules.
