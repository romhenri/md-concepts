# Anthropology

## Definition
Anthropology is the study of humans, past and present, encompassing human biology, culture, society, and language across all periods of human existence.

## Key Idea
Anthropology seeks a holistic understanding of what it means to be human, combining biological, cultural, historical, and linguistic perspectives to explain human diversity and commonality across time and place.

## Details

### Subfields
- **Cultural Anthropology** - studies human societies, beliefs, practices, and social structures.
- **Biological (Physical) Anthropology** - studies human biological evolution, genetics, and variation, connecting closely to [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]] and [[natural-selection|Natural Selection]].
- **Archaeology** - studies past human societies through material remains (artifacts, structures), often complementing evidence from [[paleontology|Paleontology]].
- **Linguistic Anthropology** - studies language in its social and cultural context.

### Methods
- **Ethnography** - immersive, firsthand observation and participation within a community.
- **Comparative Analysis** - comparing cultural practices and social structures across different societies.
- **Fieldwork** - long-term, in-context data collection, often through participant observation.

## Significance
- Provides insight into human cultural diversity and the universal patterns underlying different societies.
- Bridges the natural sciences and humanities by connecting human biological evolution to cultural and social development.
- Informs fields such as sociology, linguistics, history, and public policy.

## Limitations

### Criticisms
- Historically entangled with colonialism, sometimes portraying studied cultures through a biased outsider lens.
- Ethnographic methods can be time-intensive and difficult to generalize beyond the specific community studied.
- Balancing scientific objectivity with cultural sensitivity remains an ongoing methodological challenge.

## Related Concepts
- [[natural-evolution|Natural Evolution]] underlies the biological anthropology subfield, explaining human origins and biological diversity.
- [[paleontology|Paleontology]] often complements archaeological evidence when studying early human ancestors.
- [[taxonomy|Taxonomy]] provides the classification framework anthropologists use to place humans within the broader tree of life.
